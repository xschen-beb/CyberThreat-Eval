# Mexican Banks and Cryptocurrency Platforms Targeted With AllaKore RAT

# Enriched Doc (enrihcments marked with *content*(link)): 
 Incident: Mexican Banks and Cryptocurrency Platforms Targeted With AllaKore RAT 

 Root cause: The root cause includes spear-phishing and drive-by download attacks that exploit user vulnerability in running malicious file attachments. The payload is a modified version of the open-source AllaKore RAT, customized for financial fraud. *The attackers use deceptive tactics, employing naming conventions from the Mexican Social Security Institute (IMSS) and links to seemingly harmless documents* (https://redpiranha.net/news/threat-intelligence-report-january-30-february-5-2024). The malware is delivered via *a Microsoft software installer file* (https://cointelegraph.com/news/blackberry-mexican-crypto-exchanges-cyberthreat). *BlackBerry's Research and Intelligence Team flagged this threat, noting the malware sends stolen banking details to a command-and-control (C2) server* (https://cryptonews.com/news/blackberry-warns-mexican-bank-and-crypto-firms-on-potential-security-threat/). 

 Threat Actor/group/campaign: The threat actor is financially motivated and believed to be based in Latin America. The campaign utilizes Spanish-language instructions and infrastructure suggesting a local origin. *Numerous Mexico Starlink IPs are used, enhancing the campaign's local appearance* (https://redpiranha.net/news/threat-intelligence-report-january-30-february-5-2024). 

 Organization/industry/location: The targeted organizations include banks, cryptocurrency trading platforms, and large companies in Mexico, particularly those with gross revenues over $100M USD. *Additionally, sectors such as retail, agriculture, public sector, manufacturing, transportation, commercial services, and capital goods are also targeted* (https://cointelegraph.com/news/blackberry-mexican-crypto-exchanges-cyberthreat). *The Mercian Social Security Institute (IMSS) is also directly involved due to the use of its links and naming conventions* (https://cryptonews.com/news/blackberry-warns-mexican-bank-and-crypto-firms-on-potential-security-threat/). 

 Start date – End date: The campaign has been ongoing since at least late 2021 and continues to be active.
