However, it appears that the incident involves *MGM Resorts* (https://www.zdnet.com/article/mgm-resorts-hit-by-ransomware-attack/). MGM Resorts has shut down IT systems after a cyberattack on *September 10, 2023*, impacting *online reservations, in-casino services, and the MGM Rewards app* (https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/mgm-resorts-shuts-down-it-systems-after-cyberattack/). This includes services like ATMs, slot machines, credit card machines, Wi-Fi, and elevators, which have all been affected. The MGM Resorts main website is also down, and manual operations are in place. Employees have been *locked out of corporate email accounts* and forced to issue physical receipts and room keys. This is the second confirmed cybersecurity incident since 2019, when a breach of the company's cloud services led to the theft of over 10 million customer records. The nature of the current incident and the attacker's purpose remain unknown. *The attack was initiated by a criminal gang known as Scattered Spider, which employed social engineering tactics to gain access to the Okta tenant. BlackCat/ALPHV ransomware was used to encrypt several hundred ESXi servers, causing significant operational disruptions. MGM Resorts is losing as much as $8.4 million in revenue daily due to the attack* (https://www.cyberark.com/resources/blog/the-mgm-resorts-attack-initial-analysis). The attack has significantly impacted properties in *Las Vegas* with guests standing in hours-long lines to check-in and receive *manual mode* operations (https://passpack.com/mgm-security-breach-analysis/).
