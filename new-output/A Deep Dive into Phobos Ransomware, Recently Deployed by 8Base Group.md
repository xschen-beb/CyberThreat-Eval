# A Deep Dive into Phobos Ransomware, Recently Deployed by 8Base Group

# Enriched Doc (enrihcments marked with *content*(link)): 
 Incident: Phobos Ransomware Attack by 8Base Group 

 Root cause: The Phobos ransomware is typically deployed through SmokeLoader, a backdoor trojan that decrypts and loads the ransomware payload into its memory. The ransomware contains advanced features such as UAC bypass, persistent configurations, and the ability to disable system recovery and backups. *Phobos is also distributed via hacked Remote Desktop (RDP) connections, a cost efficient dissemination vector for threat groups* (https://malpedia.caad.fkie.fraunhofer.de/details/win.phobos). *Phobos ransomware operates in conjunction with various open source tools such as Cobalt Strike and Bloodhound* (https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa24-060a). *Phobos is an evolution of Dharma/Crysis ransomware and may be sold as a Ransomware-as-a-Service (RaaS), managed by a central authority with a single decryption key* (https://blog.talosintelligence.com/understanding-the-phobos-affiliate-structure/). *8Base ransomware operators use a variant of Phobos ransomware with the ransomware component embedded in its encrypted payloads. The ransomware component is then decrypted and loaded into the SmokeLoader process' memory* (https://securityaffairs.com/154383/malware/8base-ransomware-phobos-ransomware.html). 

 Threat Actor/group/campaign: 8Base group 

 Organization/industry/location: Not specified *; however, targeted sectors include municipal and county governments, emergency services, education, public healthcare, and other critical infrastructure entities* (https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa24-060a). *The group targets small and medium-size businesses in multiple industries, including finance, manufacturing, business services, and IT* (https://securityaffairs.com/154383/malware/8base-ransomware-phobos-ransomware.html). 

 Start date – End date: Not specified *; Phobos incidents have been regularly reported since May 2019* (https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa24-060a). *8Base group has been active since March 2022* (https://securityaffairs.com/154383/malware/8base-ransomware-phobos-ransomware.html). 

 MITRE TTPs: ['T1071.001: Application Layer Protocol - Web Protocols', 'T1059.001: Command and Scripting Interpreter - PowerShell', 'T1070.004: Indicator Removal on Host - File Deletion', 'T1027: Obfuscated Files or Information', 'T1055.012: Process Injection - Process Hollowing', 'T1082: System Information Discovery', 'T1105: Ingress Tool Transfer', 'T1490: Inhibit System Recovery', '*T1598: Phishing for Information* (https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa24-060a)', '*T1595.001: Scanning IP Blocks* (https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa24-060a)', '*T1110: Brute Force* (https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa24-060a)', '*T1133: External Remote Services* (https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa24-060a)'] 

 Impact: Not explicitly mentioned, however, the ransomware is designed to encrypt files and demand ransom, potentially causing significant disruption and financial losses to the victims. *Phobos ransomware also pivots towards double extortion, launching new leak site to further pressure victims* (https://malpedia.caad.fkie.fraunhofer.de/details/win.phobos). *Incidents have successfully ransomed several million U.S. dollars* (https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa24-060a). *Phobos affiliates, including variants like Eking, Eight, Elbie, Devos, and Faust, commonly target high-value servers to increase damage and payout chances* (https://blog.talosintelligence.com/understanding-the-phobos-affiliate-structure/). *Most victims of 8Base group are in the U.S. and Brazil* (https://securityaffairs.com/154383/malware/8base-ransomware-phobos-ransomware.html).
