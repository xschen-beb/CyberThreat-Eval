# StopRansomware ALPHV Blackcat

# Enriched Doc (enrihcments marked with *content*(link)): 
 Incident: ALPHV Blackcat Ransomware Attack 

 Root cause: The root cause includes compromised accounts, use of legitimate remote access tools exploited by ALPHV Blackcat affiliates, and vulnerable or misconfigured services like remote access software. The attack vector also involved *malvertising techniques used to deliver the Nitrogen malware* (https://darktrace.com/blog/no-bad-luck-for-darktrace-combatting-alphv-blackcat-ransomware). *ALPHV Blackcat, also known as Noberus, frequently utilizes PowerShell Scripts and Windows Task Scheduler to deploy malicious Group Policy Objects* (https://blogs.infoblox.com/threat-intelligence/dns-early-detection-breaking-the-blackcat-ransomware-kill-chain/) *and has links to the defunct DarkSide and BlackMatter groups* (https://www.akamai.com/glossary/what-is-blackcat-ransomware#:~:text=BlackCat%20ransomware%2C%20also%20known%20as,groups%20that%20are%20now%20defunct.). 

 Threat Actor/group/campaign: ALPHV Blackcat Ransomware as a Service (RaaS); *linked to Russian threat actors* (https://blogs.infoblox.com/threat-intelligence/dns-early-detection-breaking-the-blackcat-ransomware-kill-chain/); *operates with a new variant called 'Sphynx'* (https://www.akamai.com/glossary/what-is-blackcat-ransomware#:~:text=BlackCat%20ransomware%2C%20also%20known%20as,groups%20that%20are%20now%20defunct.). 

 Organization/industry/location: The healthcare sector has been the most commonly victimized, with nearly 70 leaked victims since mid-December 2023. 

 Start date – End date: Mid-December 2023 – February 27, 2024 

 MITRE TTPs: ['Phishing for Information (T1598)', 'Compromise Accounts (T1586)', 'Obtain Credentials from Passwords Stores (T1555)', 'Steal or Forge Kerberos Tickets (T1558)', 'Adversary-in-the-Middle (T1557)', 'Use of tools like Brute Ratel C4 (S1063), Cobalt Strike (S0154), and Evilginx2', '*Configure Malicious Group Policy Objects (T1484)* (https://blogs.infoblox.com/threat-intelligence/dns-early-detection-breaking-the-blackcat-ransomware-kill-chain/)'] 

 Impact: Nearly 70 victims in the healthcare sector were impacted, with data exfiltration and encryption incidents. 

 Mitigation: ['Implement application controls to manage and control the execution of software, including allowlisting remote access programs.', 'Enable and enforce multifactor authentication with strong passwords, preferably FIDO/WebAuthn or PKI-based MFA.', 'Regularly educate users on identifying suspicious emails and links through user training on social engineering and phishing attacks.', 'Monitor internal mail and messaging traffic to identify suspicious activity.', 'Install and maintain antivirus software from reputable vendors and keep it up to date.'] 

 Detailed Steps for mitigation: ['Secure remote access tools by applying allowlisting and application control solutions.', "Apply CISA's recommendations in the 'Guide to Securing Remote Access Software.'", 'Implement phishing-resistant MFA as per CISA’s guidelines.', 'Use network monitoring tools to log and report all network traffic, including lateral movement activities.', 'Regularly train users on recognizing and reporting phishing attempts.', 'Monitor internal communications for suspicious activities and establish a baseline for normal network traffic.', 'Utilize free security tools provided by CISA to prevent cyber threat actors from redirecting users to malicious websites.', 'Ensure antivirus software is installed, maintained, and updated regularly.']

 Additional Information: ['*The BlackCat ransomware, which is written in Rust, employs double extortion techniques, involving both encryption and data theft.* (https://cloudsek.com/blog/technical-analysis-of-alphv-blackcat-ransomware)', '*It uses privilege escalation to bypass User Account Control (UAC) and employs a .NET stealer tool named ExMatter, developed by the same APT group, to download files from the victim machine.* (https://cloudsek.com/blog/technical-analysis-of-alphv-blackcat-ransomware)', '*The FBI released a decryption tool for the ransomware strain in December 2023* (https://darktrace.com/blog/no-bad-luck-for-darktrace-combatting-alphv-blackcat-ransomware), and the *US Department of State announced a reward of up to USD 10 million for information leading to the identification or location of key leaders of the ALPHV group in February 2024* (https://darktrace.com/blog/no-bad-luck-for-darktrace-combatting-alphv-blackcat-ransomware).', '*The CISA issued a StopRansomware alert for ALPHV Blackcat on February 22, 2024* (https://blogs.infoblox.com/threat-intelligence/dns-early-detection-breaking-the-blackcat-ransomware-kill-chain/)', '*Infoblox identified and blocked key Blackcat C&C domains weeks to months before OSINT availability* (https://blogs.infoblox.com/threat-intelligence/dns-early-detection-breaking-the-blackcat-ransomware-kill-chain/)', '*BlackCat employs triple extortion tactics, demanding ransom for decryption, non-publication of stolen data, and to avoid DoS/DDoS attacks* (https://www.akamai.com/glossary/what-is-blackcat-ransomware#:~:text=BlackCat%20ransomware%2C%20also%20known%20as,groups%20that%20are%20now%20defunct.)']
