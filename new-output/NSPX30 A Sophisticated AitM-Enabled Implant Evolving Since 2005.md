# NSPX30 A Sophisticated AitM-Enabled Implant Evolving Since 2005

# Enriched Doc (enrihcments marked with *content*(link)): 
 Incident: NSPX30: A sophisticated AitM-enabled implant evolving since 2005. *ESET researchers* (https://dailysecurityreview.com/cyber-security/blackwood-hackers-use-aitm-to-hijack-wps-office-update-and-install-nspx30-malware/). *The implant exploits Jenkins server vulnerabilities including CVE-2024-23897* (https://mandos.io/newsletter/brief-36-apt29-targets-hpe-microsoft-blackwood-apt-nspx30-menace-more/). 

 Root cause: The root cause is the adversary-in-the-middle (AitM) attack that hijacks update requests from legitimate software such as Tencent QQ, WPS Office, and Sogou Pinyin. Attackers use packet interception to deliver the NSPX30 implant via software updates, hiding their command and control servers. *The NSPX30's modern variant evolved from Project Wood and does not rely on DNS spoofing, instead utilizing a network implant within routers* (https://www.packetlabs.net/posts/blackwood-apt-uses-aitm-attacks-to-target-software-updates/). 

 Threat Actor/group/campaign: Blackwood, a China-aligned APT group. *Blackwood possibly shares access with other Chinese APT groups, such as Evasive Panda, LuoYu, and LittleBear* (https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/blackwood-hackers-hijack-wps-office-update-to-install-malware/). 

 Organization/industry/location: Victims include Chinese and Japanese companies, individuals in China, Japan, and the UK, a high-profile UK public research university, a large Chinese manufacturing and trading company, and a Japanese corporation's engineering and manufacturing office in China. 

 Start date – End date: The implant evolution traced back to 2005, with significant activity since 2018. *Blackwood APT has been active since at least 2018* (https://www.packetlabs.net/posts/blackwood-apt-uses-aitm-attacks-to-target-software-updates/). A surge of malicious activity was detected in 2020. *NSPX30 evolved from DCM (Dark Specter), first seen in 2008* (https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/blackwood-hackers-hijack-wps-office-update-to-install-malware/).
