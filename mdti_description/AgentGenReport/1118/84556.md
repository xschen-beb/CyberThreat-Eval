#### Incident: Distribution of LummaC2 Infostealer 

#### Root cause 
 The root cause involves the distribution of LummaC2 Infostealer disguised as legitimate programs. Threat actors insert malware into legitimate files, making detection difficult. *The changes* (https://asec.ahnlab.com/en/84556/) Recently, a malware strain disguised as well-known Korean software was distributed by inserting malware into a global audio edit program, using the version information and signature of a Korean audio player program, and the icon of a Korean open-source image editing program. *Additionally*, LummaC2 has been distributed through cracked or fake popular software like VLC or ChatGPT, and spear-phishing emails impersonating well-known companies such as Bandai Namco. *Moreover, KrakenLabs team revealed that LummaC2 uses API hashing techniques for obfuscation and has been sold in underground forums since December 2022, with a subscription-based model now being more prevalent (https://outpost24.com/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-lummac2-stealer/)* (https://darktrace.com/blog/the-rise-of-the-lumma-info-stealer). *LummaC2 is also distributed through keygens, illegal cracks, and phishing campaigns, often bundled with additional malware like RedLine Stealer and Amadey Loader. It has been observed spreading SectopRAT payload via the Amadey Bot malware. *Additionally, Lumma Stealer is distributed through counterfeit websites posing as legitimate antivirus software, and automated processes for malware collection and analysis are in place at AhnLab* (https://www.quorumcyber.com/malware-reports/lummac2-stealware-report/) (https://www.cyfirma.com/research/lumma-stealer-tactics-impact-and-defense-strategies/) (https://medium.com/@harboot/efficient-distribution-of-lummac2-infostealer-via-legitimate-programs-8b14a3bbaa7b). 

#### Threat actor/group/campaign 
 The specific threat actors are not named. The campaign involves distributing LummaC2 Infostealer, *The changes* (https://asec.ahnlab.com/en/84556/) modifying legitimate files to increase infection chances. *The infostealer is believed to have been developed by the threat actor “Shamel”, under the alias “Lumma”* (https://darktrace.com/blog/the-rise-of-the-lumma-info-stealer). *The malware is available as Stealer-as-a-Service*. *The primary threat actor appears to be of Russian origin, selling the malware on Telegram and a dedicated website* (https://www.quorumcyber.com/malware-reports/lummac2-stealware-report/) (https://www.cyfirma.com/research/lumma-stealer-tactics-impact-and-defense-strategies/).

 The campaign targets individuals and organizations downloading seemingly legitimate software from untrusted sources. *The changes* (https://asec.ahnlab.com/en/84556/) Specific targets include users of global audio edit programs and Korean software. *Reported activities have been observed across multiple customer deployments mostly in the EMEA region, but also in the US* (https://darktrace.com/blog/the-rise-of-the-lumma-info-stealer).

#### Impact 
 The impact includes theft of sensitive information such as account credentials, email information, cryptocurrency wallet information, and auto-login program information. This data can be traded on the dark web or used for additional attacks, leading to secondary damage. *The changes* (https://asec.ahnlab.com/en/84556/) Breaches where corporate systems are attacked due to stolen information from personal PCs are consistently occurring. *Lumma is known to target Windows operating systems and at least 10 different browsers including Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, and Mozilla Firefox, along with crypto wallets like Binance and Ethereum* (https://darktrace.com/blog/the-rise-of-the-lumma-info-stealer). *It also targets hardware ID, CPU, RAM, and can exfiltrate files from two-factor authentication extensions* (https://www.quorumcyber.com/malware-reports/lummac2-stealware-report/). *The malware collects a wide array of data including financial data, sensitive files, FTP client data, and employs event-controlled write operations and encryption to evade detection* (https://www.cyfirma.com/research/lumma-stealer-tactics-impact-and-defense-strategies/).
